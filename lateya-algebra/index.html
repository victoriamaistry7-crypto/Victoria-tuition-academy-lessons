FOUNDATIONS

Read the expression before you touch it

5x²
Term

A piece separated by + or −.

5
Coefficient

The number multiplying a variable.

7
Constant

A number without a variable.

Example: 5x² − 3x + 7

How many terms?3
Coefficient of x²?5
Coefficient of x?−3
Constant?7
LIKE TERMS

Only identical variable parts can combine

Can combine

3x + 8x = 11x

Both terms are measured in the same “unit”: x.

Think 3 apples + 8 apples = 11 apples.

Cannot combine

3x + 8x²

x and x² are not the same algebraic object.

The power is part of the identity of the term.

Simplify

7x² + 4x − 2x² + 9x − 5

= 5x² + 13x − 5

EXPANSION

The number outside multiplies every term inside

3(x + 4)
1
Multiply 3 × x
= 3x
2
Multiply 3 × 4
= 12
3
Write the result
3x + 12

Sign alert

−2(3x − 5)

−2 × 3x = −6x

−2 × −5 = +10

So: −6x + 10

Common mistake: forgetting that negative × negative = positive
EXPANDING TWO BRACKETS

Every term in the first bracket must multiply every term in the second

Expand (x + 3)(x + 5)

1
x(x + 5) = x² + 5x
2
3(x + 5) = 3x + 15
3
Combine like terms: x² + 8x + 15
PATTERN RECOGNITION

Some expansions have patterns worth recognising

Square of a binomial

(x + a)²

= x² + 2ax + a²

Important: (x + 3)² ≠ x² + 9

Difference of squares product

(x − a)(x + a)

= x² − a²

The middle terms cancel each other.

EXPONENTS

An exponent is repeated multiplication

x⁴ = x × x × x × x

Base

x

What is being multiplied

Exponent

4

How many factors of x

Value idea

2⁴ = 16

2×2×2×2

If you understand repeated multiplication, the exponent laws stop feeling random.
EXPONENT LAWS

Use the law that matches the operation

Multiply same base

aᵐaⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ

Add exponents.

Divide same base

aᵐ/aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ

Subtract exponents.

Power of a power

(aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ

Multiply exponents.

WHY?

x³ × x² = (x×x×x)(x×x) = x⁵

There are five factors of x, so the exponents add.

EXPONENTS

Zero and negative exponents

Zero exponent

a⁰ = 1

For a ≠ 0.

a³/a³ = a³⁻³ = a⁰

But any non-zero number divided by itself is 1.

Negative exponent

a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ

Negative exponents do not mean the answer itself is negative.

x⁻³ = 1/x³

FACTORISATION

Factorisation is reverse expansion

EXPAND

3(x + 2)

↓

3x + 6

FACTORISE

3x + 6

↓

3(x + 2)

Best checking method: expand your factorised answer. If you get the original expression back, your factorisation is correct.
FACTORISATION • COMMON FACTOR

Always check for a common factor first

12x² + 18x

1
Greatest number that divides 12 and 18 → 6
2
Both terms contain x → take out x
3
12x² + 18x = 6x(2x + 3)
FACTORISATION • DIFFERENCE OF SQUARES

Two squares with a minus sign between them

a² − b² = (a − b)(a + b)

Example

x² − 25

25 = 5²

(x − 5)(x + 5)

Not a difference of squares

x² + 25

The sign is +, not −.

Do not force a pattern where it does not exist.
FACTORISATION • TRINOMIALS

Find two numbers that satisfy two conditions

Factorise x² + 7x + 12

We need two numbers that:

ADD to 7

3 + 4 = 7

MULTIPLY to 12

3 × 4 = 12

(x + 3)(x + 4)

The signs matter. For x² − 7x + 12, you need two negative numbers: −3 and −4.

EQUATIONS

An equation is a balance, not a collection of magic “move across” rules

Meaning: If you subtract 5 on the left, subtract 5 on the right. If you divide by 3 on one side, divide by 3 on the other.
SOLVING LINEAR EQUATIONS

Undo operations in a logical order

4x + 7 = 31

1
Subtract 7 from both sides → 4x = 24
2
Divide both sides by 4 → x = 6
3
Check: 4(6)+7 = 31 ✓
EQUATIONS WITH BRACKETS

You can expand first — or simplify intelligently

Method A: expand

3(x − 2) = 15

3x − 6 = 15

3x = 21

x = 7

Method B: divide first

3(x − 2) = 15

x − 2 = 5

x = 7

Both are correct because both preserve equality.

EQUATIONS WITH FRACTIONS

Clear fractions by multiplying every term by the denominator

Solve

x/3 + 2 = 6

x/3 = 4

x = 12

For more complicated fraction equations, multiply every term by the lowest common denominator.
INEQUALITIES

Same balance logic — with one critical exception

Normal operation

2x + 3 < 11

2x < 8

x < 4

Divide by a negative?

−3x > 12

Divide by −3 and reverse the sign.

x < −4

APPLICATIONS

Translate words into algebra before solving

Problem

“Three more than twice a number is 17. Find the number.”

1
Let the number be x.
2
Twice the number → 2x.
3
Three more → 2x + 3.
4
“is 17” means equals 17 → 2x + 3 = 17.
5
Solve → x = 7.
EXAM STRATEGY

The 5-question checklist before you write anything

  1. What type of algebra is this?
  2. Are there like terms?
  3. Are there brackets to expand?
  4. Can I factorise first?
  5. How will I check my answer?

Common exam traps

• Dropping a negative sign

• Combining unlike terms

• Forgetting to multiply every term in a bracket

• Not taking out the common factor first

• Forgetting to reverse an inequality sign after dividing by a negative
