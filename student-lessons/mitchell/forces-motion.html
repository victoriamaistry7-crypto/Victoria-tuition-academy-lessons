Forces & Motion

Mitchell's interactive lesson: vectors, resultant force, balanced and unbalanced forces, and force diagrams.

1. Force basics

A force is a push or pull that can change an object's motion, direction, or shape. Force is measured in newtons (N).

A force can...

start or stop motion, change speed, change direction, or change shape.

Key idea

Force has both magnitude and direction.

Quick check: Which best describes a force?

2. Scalars vs vectors

A scalar has magnitude only. A vector has magnitude and direction.

Scalars

distance, speed, time, mass

Vectors

displacement, velocity, force, acceleration

Exam rule: when direction matters, “10 N” is incomplete. Write “10 N right” or show the direction with an arrow.
Quick check: Which is a vector?

3. Resultant force

The resultant (net) force is the single overall force produced by all the forces acting together.

Fresultant = force one way − force the opposite way

Same direction: add. Opposite directions: subtract and keep the direction of the larger force.

Worked example 1

12 N right and 5 N left

12 − 5 = 7 N right
Worked example 2

18 N right and 7 N right

18 + 7 = 25 N right
Worked example 3

20 N right + 8 N right − 11 N left

28 − 11 = 17 N right

4. Balanced vs unbalanced

Balanced

Resultant force = 0 N. Motion does not change. The object may be at rest or move at constant velocity.

Unbalanced

Resultant force ≠ 0 N. Velocity changes: speed, direction, or both.

Important: balanced forces do not automatically mean “stationary”. A moving object can have a resultant of 0 N.
Quick check: A cyclist moves straight at constant velocity. Which is best?

6. Exam method

Method: identify directions → combine forces → calculate resultant → state direction → explain the motion.
Model answer

24 N east and 16 N west:

24 − 16 = 8 N east

The forces are unbalanced because the resultant force is 8 N east. Therefore, the crate accelerates east.

Avoid: adding opposite forces, omitting direction, calling non-zero resultant forces balanced, or assuming every moving object has an unbalanced force.
