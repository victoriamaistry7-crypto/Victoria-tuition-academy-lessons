A friendly, visual lesson on what common acne lesions look like, what is happening inside the pore, what treatments are meant to do, and how to care for acne-prone skin without destroying your barrier.

Lesson 1

First: identify what you’re actually looking at

Different bumps can need different strategies. These are simplified educational illustrations — real skin varies in colour, size and appearance.

Whitehead / closed comedone

A small flesh-coloured or pale bump. The clogged pore is closed at the surface.

Clogged poreOften non-inflamed

Blackhead / open comedone

The pore is clogged but stays open. The dark colour is oxidation — not dirt.

Open clogDo not scrub

Papule

A red, inflamed bump without a visible pus-filled centre. It can feel tender or firm.

Inflamed

Pustule

An inflamed pimple with a visible white or yellow centre containing pus.

InflamedDon’t pop

Nodule / cystic-type breakout

A deeper, often painful lump under the skin. These have a higher risk of scarring.

Deep + painfulDermatologist territory

PIH: the flat dark mark after acne

If it is flat and only the colour changed, it may be post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation rather than a true scar.

PigmentCan fade
Lesson 2

What is happening underneath the skin?

Acne is not proof that your face is dirty. It forms inside the hair follicle when oil, shedding skin cells, microbes and inflammation interact.

Simplified cross-section for learning; not to scale.

1
Sebum is normal.Your oil glands make sebum to protect the skin. Acne-prone follicles are more likely to become obstructed.
2
Cells + oil form a plug.This can become a whitehead or blackhead depending on whether the pore opening is closed or open.
3
Inflammation changes the picture.A blocked follicle can become red, swollen and painful, producing papules, pustules or deeper lesions.
Lesson 3

Match the treatment to the problem

You do not need every active. Pick the treatment that addresses what your skin is doing, then give it enough time to work.

Whiteheads + blackheads

Think: clogged follicles.

Useful: salicylic acid and topical retinoids such as adapalene can help unclog pores and prevent new comedones.
Avoid: squeezing or abrasive scrubs.
Red papules + pustules

Think: clogging + inflammation.

Useful: benzoyl peroxide is especially useful for inflammatory acne; salicylic acid can also help some people.
Avoid: piling multiple harsh treatments onto irritated skin.
Acne + dark marks

Think: acne control + pigment control.

Useful: azelaic acid can target acne and post-acne discoloration. Daily broad-spectrum sunscreen matters.
Avoid: picking — trauma increases the chance of a lasting mark.
Deep painful nodules/cysts

Think: deeper disease + scar risk.

Best next step: see a dermatologist. Store-bought skincare is often not enough for deep, scarring acne.
Never: attempt to lance or squeeze a deep lesion yourself.
Lesson 4

Your routine should be boring in the best way

The strongest acne routine is one you can tolerate and repeat. Clean gently, use a targeted treatment, moisturise, and protect from UV.

☀️ Morning

1
Gentle cleanse or rinse
You are removing excess oil and residue, not stripping the barrier.
2
Optional hydrating layer
Glycerin, betaine, sodium PCA, panthenol, hyaluronic acid or amino acids can help with dehydration.
3
Moisturiser
Acne-prone skin still needs water retention and barrier support.
4
Broad-spectrum SPF 30+
Especially important when you are treating post-acne marks.

🌙 Night

1
Gentle cleanse
Remove sunscreen, makeup, sweat and excess oil.
2
One main acne treatment
For example: azelaic acid, benzoyl peroxide, salicylic acid or a retinoid — chosen for your needs.
3
Hydration if needed
A light humectant layer can help if treatment makes you tight or flaky.
4
Moisturiser
Seal in hydration and support your barrier overnight.
Lesson 5

Know the job of each ingredient

A product is useful when its formula performs a job you actually need. These are the ingredient families worth recognising.

HydrateGlycerin

Reliable humectant that helps the outer skin hold water.

HydrateBetaine / Sodium PCA

Help maintain water balance without needing a heavy cream.

BarrierDimethicone

Smooths skin and reduces water loss. Silicone is not automatically pore-clogging.

BarrierCeramides + cholesterol + fatty acids

Components used to support the lipid barrier.

UnclogSalicylic acid

BHA used for clogged pores, blackheads and whiteheads.

InflammationBenzoyl peroxide

Reduces acne-associated bacteria and helps inflammatory acne.

Multi-taskAzelaic acid

Useful for acne, inflammation and post-acne pigmentation.

Prevent clogsAdapalene / retinoids

Normalize follicular cell turnover and help prevent future clogged pores.

SoothePanthenol / allantoin

Useful supportive ingredients when skin is irritated or dehydrated.

BrightenNiacinamide

Can support barrier function and uneven tone. Higher percentages are not automatically better.

ProtectUV filters

Broad-spectrum sunscreen helps prevent dark marks from becoming more persistent.

Be cautiousHarsh scrubs + too many acids

Irritation can make acne care harder and can worsen discoloration from picking or inflammation.

Lesson 6

Acne habits that quietly sabotage progress

🫧
Washing more is not the answer.Gentle cleansing up to twice daily is usually enough. Repeated harsh washing can irritate skin.
✋
Picking creates extra inflammation.That increases your chance of post-acne discoloration and scarring.
🧪
Do not start five actives together.If your barrier becomes irritated, you will not know which product caused it.
⏳
Give treatment time.Meaningful acne improvement usually takes weeks, not two nights.
💧
Oily skin can still be dehydrated.Oil is not the same thing as water. Acne treatment often becomes easier to tolerate with appropriate moisturisation.
🧴
Watch what touches the hairline.Oily hair products can contribute to tiny forehead/hairline breakouts in some people.
Lesson 7

Know when skincare needs medical backup

See a clinician sooner if acne is deep, painful or scarring.

Also get medical advice if breakouts are widespread, suddenly change, do not improve despite a consistent evidence-based routine, or are seriously affecting how you feel. Deep nodules and cysts have a higher scar risk and often need prescription treatment.

Urgent reaction? Significant facial swelling, blistering, breathing difficulty or rapidly worsening severe skin symptoms need prompt medical assessment rather than another skincare experiment.
