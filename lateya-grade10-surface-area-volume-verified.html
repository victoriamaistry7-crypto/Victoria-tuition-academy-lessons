Section 01 · 0–10 min

Know what you're calculating

Before using a formula, identify what the question is actually asking for: the outside covering or the space inside.

SURFACE AREA (SA)

Outside covering

Sum of all exposed faces of a 3D solid. Think: paper needed to wrap a gift.

cm2, m2, mm2
VOLUME (V)

Space inside

How much space a solid occupies. Think: water that fills a tank.

cm3, m3, mm3

3D solids have length, width and/or height. Always write units. Diagrams are schematic, not drawn to scale.

Conversions to remember

1 cm3 = 1 mL  ·  1 000 cm3 = 1 L

1 m3 = 1 000 000 cm3

Area uses squared conversions; volume uses cubed conversions. Never confuse the two.

Quick check A

The amount of paint needed to cover a box is measured in cm?. Enter the power.

Paint covers the outside, so the answer is cm². The power is 2.

Quick check B

Convert 3 500 cm3 to litres.

3 500 ÷ 1 000 = 3,5 L.
Section 02 · 10–25 min

Rectangular & triangular prisms

A prism has the same cross-section all the way along its length. Volume = cross-sectional area × length.

RECTANGULAR PRISM
V = ℓwh
SA = 2(ℓw + ℓh + wh)

Six faces: 2 matching top/bottom, 2 front/back and 2 left/right.

Worked example 1 · A box is 8 cm long, 5 cm wide and 3 cm high. Calculate both volume and total surface area.
V = 8 × 5 × 3 = 120 cm³SA = 2(8 × 5 + 8 × 3 + 5 × 3)= 2(40 + 24 + 15) = 158 cm²

Triangular prisms

V = (12bh)L
SA = 2A + PL

A = area of triangular cross-section; P = perimeter of triangle; L = prism length. The diagram matches Worked Example 2: base 6 cm, perpendicular height 4 cm, equal sides 5 cm, and length 10 cm. For triangular SA, you need all 3 triangle side lengths.

Worked example 2 · An isosceles triangular cross-section has base 6 cm, perpendicular height 4 cm, equal sides 5 cm, and prism length 10 cm.
A = 12(6)(4) = 12 cm²P = 5 + 5 + 6 = 16 cmV = 12(10) = 120 cm³SA = 2(12) + 16(10) = 184 cm²

Your turn 1

Find the volume of a rectangular prism: ℓ = 6 cm, w = 4 cm, h = 4 cm.

V = 6 × 4 × 4 = 96 cm³.

Your turn 2

A triangular prism has triangle base 7 cm, perpendicular triangle height 5 cm and length 8 cm. Find its volume.

Area = ½ × 7 × 5 = 17,5 cm². V = 17,5 × 8 = 140 cm³.
Section 03 · 25–40 min

Cylinders & open containers

A cylinder has two circular bases, plus one curved surface. The net of the curved part is a rectangle with length 2πr and height h.

V = πr2h
SA = 2πr2 + 2πrh

If diameter d is given, radius r = d ÷ 2. The 2πr² term counts both circular ends.

Worked example 3 · Cylinder with radius 3 cm and height 10 cm. Use π on the calculator.
V = π(3)2(10) = 90π = 282,74 cm³SA = 2π(3)2 + 2π(3)(10)= 18π + 60π = 78π = 245,04 cm²
COMMON EXAM TRAP
An open-top cylinder has only ONE circular base.
SAopen = πr2 + 2πrh

For a pipe open at both ends, the outer curved area is 2πrh (ignore material thickness).

Worked example 4 · Open-top tin: r = 4 cm, h = 7 cm. Find the metal needed, excluding the top.
SA = π(4)2 + 2π(4)(7)= 16π + 56π = 72π= 226,19 cm²

Your turn 3

A cylinder has diameter 10 cm and height 6 cm. Find its volume to 2 decimal places.

Radius = 10 ÷ 2 = 5 cm. V = π(5)²(6) = 150π ≈ 471,24 cm³.

Your turn 4

Calculate total surface area of a closed cylinder with r = 2 cm and h = 8 cm, to 2 decimals.

2π(2²) + 2π(2)(8) = 8π + 32π = 40π ≈ 125,66 cm².
Section 04 · 40–60 min

Cones & right pyramids

A cone and a pyramid each have one third of the volume of a corresponding cylinder/prism with the same base area and perpendicular height.

RIGHT CIRCULAR CONE
V = 13πr2h
SA = πr2 + πrℓ
ℓ = √r2 + h2

Remember: h is the perpendicular (vertical) height. ℓ is the slant height along the side. Use h for volume and ℓ for curved surface area.

Worked example 5 · A cone has r = 5 cm and h = 12 cm. Calculate total surface area and volume.
ℓ = √(5² + 12²) = √169 = 13 cmSA = π(5²) + π(5)(13) = 90π= 282,74 cm²V = 13π(5²)(12) = 100π = 314,16 cm³

Square-based right pyramids

V = 13s2h
SA = s2 + 2sℓ
ℓ = √h2 + (s2)2

SA = square base + four triangular faces. Each triangle's area is ½ × s × ℓ, hence four faces = 2sℓ. For an equilateral-triangle-based right pyramid, use V = ⅓ × (triangle base area) × perpendicular height; SA = base area plus the three triangular faces.

Worked example 6 · Square pyramid: base side = 10 cm, perpendicular height = 12 cm.
ℓ = √(12² + 5²) = √169 = 13 cmV = 13(10²)(12) = 400 cm³SA = 10² + 2(10)(13) = 360 cm²

Your turn 5

A cone has radius 3 cm and perpendicular height 4 cm. Find its volume to 2 decimals.

V = ⅓ × π × 3² × 4 = 12π ≈ 37,70 cm³.

Your turn 6

A square pyramid has base side 6 cm and perpendicular height 12 cm. Find volume.

V = ⅓ × 6² × 12 = 144 cm³.
Section 05 · 60–75 min

Spheres & hemispheres

A sphere is a perfectly round 3D object. A hemisphere is exactly half of a sphere.

COMPLETE SPHERE
V = 43πr3
SA = 4πr2
Worked example 7 · A sphere has radius 3 cm.
SA = 4π(3²) = 36π ≈ 113,10 cm²V = 43π(3³) = 36π ≈ 113,10 cm³

The numerical results are equal by coincidence; the units and meanings are different.

HEMISPHERE
V = 23πr3
Curved SA = 2πr2
Total SA = 3πr2

Why total SA is 3πr²: curved half-sphere = 2πr², and circular flat base = πr². Total = 3πr².

Worked example 8 · A hemisphere of radius 4 cm has total surface area = 3π(4²) = 48π ≈ 150,80 cm².

Your turn 7

Calculate the total surface area of a sphere with r = 4 cm, to two decimals.

SA = 4π(4²) = 64π ≈ 201,06 cm².

Your turn 8

Calculate total surface area (including the circular base) of a hemisphere with r = 5 cm.

SA = 3π(5²) = 75π ≈ 235,62 cm².
Section 06 · 75–90 min

Composite solids & scaling

Composite solids are made by joining two or more familiar solids. For volume, add (or subtract) the parts. For surface area, only count exposed surfaces.

Worked example 9 · A cylinder of radius 3 cm and height 8 cm has a hemisphere of the same radius attached on top. Calculate total volume and exposed total surface area.
V = πr²h + 23πr³= π(3²)(8) + 23π(3³)= 72π + 18π = 282,74 cm³SA = bottom circle + cylinder side + hemisphere curve= π(3²) + 2π(3)(8) + 2π(3²)= 9π + 48π + 18π = 235,62 cm²

The hidden circle where the two shapes are joined is not exposed. Do not count it.

If only one dimension changes, these general scale rules do not automatically apply. For example, doubling just a cylinder's height doubles its volume but does not double its total surface area.

Worked example 10 · If every dimension of a solid is tripled (k = 3):
New SA = 3² × original SA = 9 × original SANew V = 3³ × original V = 27 × original V

Your turn 9

All dimensions of a cube are doubled. By what factor does the volume increase?

Volume scale factor = 2³ = 8.

Your turn 10

A sphere has surface area 4 cm². Every linear dimension triples. What is its new surface area?

New SA = 3² × 4 = 36 cm².
