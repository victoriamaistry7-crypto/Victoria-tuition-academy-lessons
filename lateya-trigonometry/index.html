03 • Why trig works

Same angle.
Same ratio.

Similar right triangles have proportional corresponding sides. That is why sin, cos and tan depend on the angle—not on the physical size of the triangle.

Core idea: if θ stays the same, O/H, A/H and O/A stay the same even when every side is scaled.
04 • Triangle anatomy

θ controls O and A.
H never moves.

Move θ between the two acute angles. The opposite and adjacent sides swap roles; the hypotenuse remains opposite the right angle.

Hypotenuse: always opposite the 90° angle.

06 • Pythagoras link

Sometimes trig starts
with a missing side.

If two sides are given, first complete the right triangle with Pythagoras. Then construct the required ratio.

r² = x² + y²
Exam habit: do not reach for sin/cos/tan if the missing side can be found directly by Pythagoras.
07 • Missing side

Normal trig key.
Then isolate x.

Given θ = 35° and adjacent = 8 cm, calculate the opposite side.

tan 35° = x8
Normal trig: finding a side uses sin/cos/tan, not the inverse function.
08 • Missing angle

Inverse trig
returns the angle.

Opposite = 7 and adjacent = 10. Find θ.

θ = tan⁻¹(710)
Finding a side: normal trig. Finding an angle: inverse trig.

12 • 30°–60°–90°

See the side pattern.
Then build the ratio.

The side lengths opposite 30°, 60° and 90° are proportional to 1, √3 and 2.

13 • 45°–45°–90°

Equal acute angles.
Equal legs.

The side pattern is 1 : 1 : √2, so sin 45° and cos 45° are equal.

Without a calculator, simplify sin 45° ÷ cos 45°.

17 • Reference angle

Reduce to the acute
angle against the x-axis.

Move θ. The reference angle is always the positive acute angle between the terminal arm and the x-axis.

θ140°
Reference angle = 40°
Examples: QII → 180°−θ; QIII → θ−180°; QIV → 360°−θ.
18 • Coordinate definition

Point P(x,y) gives
all six ratios.

For a terminal point P(x,y), first find r = √(x²+y²). Then build the ratios from x, y and r.

sin θ = y/r   cos θ = x/r   tan θ = y/x
cosec θ = r/y   sec θ = r/x   cot θ = x/y
19 • One ratio → the others

Build a triangle
in the correct quadrant.

If sin θ = −3/5 and θ lies in quadrant III, y is negative and r is positive. Use Pythagoras to find x and the quadrant to choose its sign.

Determine cos θ without a calculator.
Use y = −3, r = 5. Then x² = 25−9 = 16. In QIII, x is negative, so x = −4 and cos θ = x/r = −4/5.

22 • Notation trap

2 sin θ is not
sin 2θ.

CAPS explicitly warns that doubling the ratio and doubling the angle are different operations.

At θ = 30°, which statement is true?
Watch: sin²θ means (sinθ)². sin(2θ) means take sine of the doubled angle.

27 • Equation from a graph

Read amplitude
and vertical shift.

A graph has maximum 3 and minimum −1. Find a and q in y = a sin x + q.

Which equation fits?
Amplitude = (max−min)/2 = (3−(−1))/2 = 2. Midline q = (max+min)/2 = 1.
28 • Graph interpretation

Read coordinates,
intercepts and range.

Papers often give a sketch and ask for a value, period, range, reflection or parameter rather than “draw the whole graph”.

For y = −2 cos x + 1, what is the range?
Fast method: midline = q. Distance from midline to max/min = |a|.
q = 1
|a| = 2

minimum = 1−2 = −1
maximum = 1+2 = 3
29 • Angle of elevation

Horizontal eye line
to an object above.

A learner stands 20 m from a building. The angle of elevation to the top is 40°. Find the height.

tan 40° = h20
30 • Angle of depression

Measure from the
horizontal at the observer.

The angle of depression equals the corresponding angle of elevation because the horizontal lines are parallel.

From a 30 m cliff, the angle of depression to a boat is 35°. Find the horizontal distance d.
31 • 2D problem solving

Draw, label, split,
then calculate.

Harder Grade 10 questions often combine more than one right triangle. Do one triangle at a time and carry an exact/calculator value forward.

A tower stands 18 m from point A. The angle of elevation is 52°. Find the tower height.
Check reasonableness: 52° is steep, so the opposite side should be larger than the adjacent 18 m.
