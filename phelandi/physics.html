02 • Resistance bridge

Resistance first.

Before circuit analysis, reconnect to the idea you already covered: resistance is opposition to the flow of electric charge.

Length

Longer wire → more resistance.

Thickness

Thicker wire → less resistance.

Material

Different conductors resist charge differently.

Temperature

For metals, higher temperature generally increases resistance.

Which copper wire should have the LOWEST resistance if temperature is the same?

03 • Current vs potential difference

Current and voltage are not the same thing.

Current, I

The rate at which electric charge flows past a point in a circuit. Unit: ampere (A).

Potential difference, V

Energy transferred per unit charge between two points. Unit: volt (V).

Exam trap: a cell does not “store current”. The source provides energy; current is the movement of charge in a complete circuit.
Which instrument measures current?

04 • Circuit symbols

Translate the picture into a circuit.

Before Phelandi can analyse a circuit, she must recognise the symbols quickly and know what each component does.

Cell / battery

Provides electrical energy to the circuit.

Switch

Opens or closes the conducting path.

Resistor / lamp

Transfers energy and offers resistance to current.

A and V meters

Ammeter measures current; voltmeter measures potential difference.

Exam habit: identify every symbol first, then trace the complete path before deciding what the circuit will do.

06 • Series circuits

One path. Same current.

In a series circuit, every charge follows the same path through each component.

Rtotal = R1 + R2 + …

Current

Same at every point in the single path.

Voltage

The source potential difference is shared across components.

Three resistors of 2 Ω, 3 Ω and 4 Ω are connected in series. Calculate total resistance.
New learning8 min
07 • Parallel circuits

Branches change the behaviour.

Parallel circuits give charges more than one conducting path.

Voltage

Each parallel branch is connected across the same two points, so branch potential difference is the same.

Current

Total current splits at a junction and recombines later.

Failure clue: if one branch opens, another branch can still form a complete circuit.
One lamp in a parallel branch burns out. What should happen to a lamp in another healthy branch?
Guided practice5 min
08 • Series vs parallel

Series or parallel? Prove it.

Series

One route. Same current. Resistances add. A break opens the only path.

Parallel

More than one route. Current divides. Branches can operate independently.

Which observation actually proves a circuit contains parallel branches?
Exam language: “The circuit is parallel because the components are connected on separate branches between the same two junctions.”
