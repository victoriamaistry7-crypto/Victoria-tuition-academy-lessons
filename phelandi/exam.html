Section A • Natural Sciences / Physics

Electric circuits & resistance

30 marks
QUESTION 1.1
Which statement best describes electric current in a complete circuit?
[2]
Teacher correction

Current is the rate of flow of electric charge and is measured in amperes (A). Potential difference describes energy per unit charge, while resistance is opposition to current.

Exam memory: Current → charge flow → ampere → ammeter.
QUESTION 1.2
A voltmeter must be connected …
[2]
Teacher correction

A voltmeter compares the electric potential at two points, so it is placed across the two terminals of the component.

Exam memory: A → series. V → parallel across the component.
QUESTION 1.3
Which change will generally increase the resistance of a metal wire?
[2]
Teacher correction

A longer wire gives moving charges a longer path through the conductor, so resistance increases when the other variables are controlled.

Relationship: longer → more resistance; thicker → less resistance.
QUESTION 1.4
Two lamps are connected on separate branches between the same two junctions. The arrangement is …
[2]
Teacher correction

Parallel components are placed on separate branches between the same pair of junctions. That gives charge more than one conducting path.

QUESTION 2.1
Three resistors of 4 Ω, 3 Ω and 5 Ω are connected in series. Calculate the total resistance.
Show your calculation on paper, then enter the final answer in ohms.
[4]
Teacher correction

In a series circuit, resistance adds because every charge must pass through every resistor.

Method: Rtotal = R1 + R2 + R3 = 4 + 3 + 5 = 12 Ω.
QUESTION 2.2
In a series circuit, an ammeter reads 0.40 A before the first resistor. What current should an ideal ammeter read after the second resistor?
[3]
Teacher correction

A series circuit has only one path. The same current passes through every point in that path.

If the current is 0.40 A at one point in an unbranched series circuit, it is 0.40 A at the other points too.
QUESTION 2.3
Two identical lamps are connected in parallel. One lamp is removed, opening only its branch. What happens to the other lamp?
[3]
Teacher correction

Parallel branches are separate paths. Opening one branch does not automatically open every other branch.

Exam wording: “The other lamp remains connected across the source on a complete branch.”
QUESTION 3.1
A learner investigates how the length of a nichrome wire affects current. The wire material and thickness are kept constant. What is the independent variable?
[3]
Teacher correction

The independent variable is the factor deliberately changed by the learner. Here, that is the length of the wire.

IV = changed deliberately. DV = measured response. Controls = kept the same.
QUESTION 3.2
Why must the thickness of the wire be kept constant in the investigation?
[3]
Teacher correction

A fair test changes one relevant factor at a time. Since thickness affects resistance, it must be controlled when testing the effect of length.

QUESTION 4
Study the simplified circuit below. Which description is correct?
[6]
Teacher correction

Trace the paths: the two lamps lie on separate branches, so they are parallel. The ammeter lies in the main path, while the voltmeter bridges the two terminals of the upper lamp.

Diagram strategy: first count paths, then locate A in the path, then check whether V spans the component.
Section B • Mathematics

Equations & straight-line graphs

30 marks
QUESTION 5.1
Solve for x: 4x − 7 = 21
[3]
Teacher correction

Undo the operations in reverse while keeping both sides balanced.

4x − 7 = 21 → 4x = 28 → x = 7. Check: 4(7) − 7 = 21.
QUESTION 5.2
Solve for x: 2(x + 4) = 18
[4]
Teacher correction

You can divide both sides by 2 first or expand the bracket first.

2(x + 4) = 18 → x + 4 = 9 → x = 5.
QUESTION 5.3
Simplify: 4a + 7 − a + 3
[2]
Teacher correction

Combine like terms only: 4a − a = 3a and 7 + 3 = 10.

Final simplified expression: 3a + 10.
QUESTION 6.1
Which coordinate represents a point 3 units to the right of the origin and 2 units below the x-axis?
[3]
Teacher correction

Coordinates are always written (x ; y). Right means positive x and below means negative y.

3 right → x = 3. 2 down → y = −2. Therefore (3 ; −2).
QUESTION 6.2
Calculate the gradient of the straight line through A(1 ; 2) and B(3 ; 8).
[4]
Teacher correction

Gradient compares the vertical change with the horizontal change.

m = (8 − 2) / (3 − 1) = 6 / 2 = 3.
QUESTION 6.3
For the line y = −3x + 4, which statement is correct?
[3]
Teacher correction

Compare the equation directly with y = mx + c.

The coefficient of x is m, so m = −3. The standalone constant is c, so c = 4.
QUESTION 7.1
The rule is y = 2x − 1. Calculate y when x = 3.
[3]
Teacher correction

Substitute the given x-value into the rule.

y = 2(3) − 1 = 6 − 1 = 5. The point is (3 ; 5).
QUESTION 7.2
Which point lies on the graph y = 2x + 1?
[3]
Teacher correction

Test a point by substituting its x-coordinate into the equation and checking whether you get its y-coordinate.

For x = 2: y = 2(2) + 1 = 5, so (2 ; 5) lies on the line.
QUESTION 7.3
A straight line has gradient 3 and y-intercept −2. Which equation represents the line?
[3]
Teacher correction

Place the gradient in front of x and the y-intercept as the final constant.

y = mx + c → y = 3x + (−2) → y = 3x − 2.
QUESTION 8
Study the graph. Which statement best describes the line?
[2]
Teacher correction

The line falls as x increases, so its gradient is negative. It crosses the y-axis above the origin, so its y-intercept is positive.
