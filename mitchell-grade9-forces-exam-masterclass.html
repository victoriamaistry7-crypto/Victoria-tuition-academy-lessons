02 / Explain and practise

What makes a force a vector?

Length shows size. Arrowhead shows direction.

7–17 min
Key idea

Every force needs 2 things

Magnitude tells us how big the force is (e.g., 6 N). Direction tells us where it acts (e.g., right).

Full vector statement: A force of 6 N to the right.

A scalar has magnitude only (e.g., mass 5 kg). A vector has magnitude and direction (e.g., force 6 N east).

Say it aloud

“A force is a vector quantity because it has both magnitude and direction.”

Spot the incomplete answer

“The boy pushed with 10 N.”

What essential information is missing?

Direction: for example 10 N to the left or 10 N east.

Vector quick-check

A force of 4 N is acting to the left. Which sketch matches?

Mini-coaching script for the tutor

Ask Mitchell to draw 5 N right, then draw 5 N left. Discuss: same magnitude; opposite directions. Next ask him to draw 10 N right using an arrow twice as long as his 5 N arrow. Exact scale matters only when the diagram says it is drawn to scale.

03 / Classify and justify

Two families of forces

Does the interaction require touching?

17–28 min
Touching

Contact forces

  • Friction: resists relative sliding or a tendency to slide between touching surfaces.
  • Tension: a pulling force along a taut rope or string.
  • Compression: a pushing or squeezing force on an object.
Without touching

Field (non-contact) forces

  • Gravitational: objects with mass attract one another.
  • Magnetic: magnets can attract or repel without touching.
  • Electrostatic: charged objects attract or repel at a distance.

Classify the force · quick game

Choose contact (C) or field (F). The question shows an explanation after each answer.

1. A ball falls toward Earth.
2. A rope pulls a bucket upward.
3. A balloon rubbed on hair attracts paper.
4. A tyre grips the road.

04 / Interactive investigation

Resultant and balanced forces

Combine forces along the same straight line.

28–43 min

Resultant force (net force)

The resultant is the single force that has the same overall effect as all the forces acting on the object.

Along one line: add forces acting in the same direction; subtract opposing forces. The resultant points toward the larger total.

Fnet = 8 N − 3 N = 5 N →

Balanced: resultant is 0 N. Motion does not change; the object can be at rest or moving at constant velocity.

Unbalanced: resultant is not 0 N. The object accelerates (changes speed and/or direction).

Net force points right: motion changes in the rightward direction.

Balanced does NOT always mean stopped

If a bicycle moves at a steady speed in a straight line, the horizontal forces can be balanced: Fnet = 0 N.

Correct exam wording: “The object stays at rest or continues at constant velocity, because the net force is zero.”

Same direction? Add

Two people push a trolley to the right:

6 N → + 4 N → = 10 N →

Opposite direction? Subtract:

12 N → − 5 N ← = 7 N →

The “−” in the second line means subtract the opposing magnitudes; include the direction in the final answer.

05 / Exam technique

Read. Diagram. Calculate. Explain.

One complete worked problem, then one for Mitchell to try.

43–50 min
Worked example · 5 marks

Question: two students pull a box

Student A pulls a box with 14 N to the right. Student B pulls with 6 N to the left. Ignore other horizontal forces.

(a) Identify a suitable unit for force. (1)

(b) Determine the resultant force, with direction. (2)

(c) State whether horizontal forces are balanced. (1)

(d) Describe the resulting change in motion. (1)

Method

1. Mark right as the positive direction.

2. Subtract the opposite force from the larger force.

3. Include newtons and direction.

4. Use the net force to decide whether forces are balanced.

(a) Newton (N). [1]
(b) 14 N − 6 N = 8 N right. [2: calculation and direction/unit]
(c) Unbalanced; 8 N ≠ 0 N. [1]
(d) Box accelerates right (if initially at rest, starts moving right). [1]
Independent exam task · 4 marks

Your turn, Mitchell

Two forces act on a trolley: 10 N to the left and 4 N to the right. Draw labelled horizontal force arrows on the trolley and calculate the resultant. Is it balanced? Explain the likely change in motion.

Force arrows: ← 10 N and 4 N → on the trolley. Resultant = 10 N − 4 N = 6 N left [2]; unbalanced [1]; trolley accelerates to the left [1]. If it was moving right initially, it slows down first. The resultant’s direction is not necessarily the existing motion direction.

Tutor-only notes and full marking guidance

Teaching checkpoints

  • Vector: both size and direction; saying “6 N” alone is incomplete.
  • Contact: friction, tension, compression; field: gravitational, magnetic, electrostatic.
  • Resultant: same direction add; opposite directions subtract; final answer must state N and direction.
  • Balanced: Fnet = 0 N, no acceleration; can be stationary or at constant velocity.
  • Newton’s third law pairs: act on different objects, and must not be mistaken for two balanced forces acting on the same object.
