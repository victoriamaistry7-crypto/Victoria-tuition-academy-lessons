TERM 1 · LIFE & LIVING

1. Cells & levels of organisation

A cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life. Plant and animal cells share a cell membrane, cytoplasm and nucleus. Typical plant cells also have a cell wall, chloroplasts and a large permanent vacuole.

Know the structures

Nucleus: contains genetic material and controls cell activities.

Cell wall: rigid support outside the plant cell membrane.

Chloroplast: contains chlorophyll; site of photosynthesis.

Vacuole: cell sap; helps maintain plant-cell shape.

Organisation hierarchy

cell→tissue→organ→organ system→organism
Exam trap: tissues are groups of similar cells working together; organs are made from different tissues working together.
TERM 1 · LIFE & LIVING

2. Human body systems & data

Four key systems

Nervous: detects stimuli and coordinates responses.

Musculoskeletal: support, protection and movement.

Excretory: removes metabolic wastes and regulates body fluids.

Circulatory: transports oxygen, nutrients and wastes.

Blood vessels

Artery: carries blood away from the heart.

Vein: carries blood toward the heart.

Capillary: tiny vessel where exchange occurs with tissues.

Memory: Artery = Away.

Heart-rate investigation

Before exercise: 72 bpm → immediately after: 132 bpm → five minutes later: 88 bpm.

The dependent variable is heart rate because it is measured. Exercise makes muscles demand more oxygen and glucose and increases carbon-dioxide removal, so the heart pumps faster. The respiratory system supplies oxygen to the blood.

TERM 1 · LIFE & LIVING

3. Reproduction & digestion

Human reproduction

Puberty: stage during which the body becomes sexually mature.

Testes produce sperm; ovaries produce ova (egg cells).

Ovulation→Fertilisation→Implantation

Menstruation: shedding of the uterine lining when pregnancy does not occur.

Placenta: exchange of oxygen, nutrients and wastes between mother and foetus.

Digestion

Mouth→Oesophagus→Stomach→Small intestine→Large intestine

Mechanical digestion physically breaks food into smaller pieces. Chemical digestion uses enzymes and digestive chemicals to form smaller soluble molecules.

Most nutrient absorption occurs in the small intestine.

Egestion: removal of undigested food.

Classify substances

Element: one type of atom. O₂ is an element because both atoms are oxygen.

Compound: different elements chemically bonded in a fixed ratio. CO₂ is a compound.

Mixture: substances physically mixed, not bonded in a fixed ratio. Air is a mixture.

Subscripts count atoms in a formula: CO₂ = 1 carbon + 2 oxygen atoms.

TERM 2 · MATTER & MATERIALS

5. Chemical reactions, oxygen & rusting

Balance without changing formulas

Atoms are conserved. Change only the coefficients in front of formulas.

2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO

Left: 2 Mg, 2 O. Right: 2 Mg, 2 O.

Word equation: magnesium + oxygen → magnesium oxide

Patterns to know

metal + oxygen → metal oxide
non-metal + oxygen → non-metal oxide

Iron rusting requires both oxygen and water. Paint protects iron by creating a barrier.

Burning fossil fuels can release sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides, which can contribute to acid rain.

Read the pH scale

pH < 7: acidic

pH = 7: neutral

pH > 7: basic/alkaline

Universal indicator: strong acid ≈ red; neutral ≈ green; strong base ≈ purple/violet.

Neutralisation & investigations

acid + metal oxide → salt + water

hydrochloric acid + magnesium oxide → magnesium chloride + water

In a limestone/acidic-soil investigation: IV = amount of limestone changed; DV = soil pH measured.

TERM 3 · ENERGY & CHANGE

7. Forces, magnetism & electrostatics

Force basics

A force is a push or pull that can change motion, direction or shape. SI unit: newton (N).

Contact forces: friction, tension.

Field/non-contact forces: gravity, magnetic force, electrostatic force.

Mass, weight & interactions

Mass: amount of matter, measured in kg; approximately constant with location.

Weight: gravitational force, measured in N; changes if gravitational field strength changes.

Like magnetic poles repel; unlike poles attract. Like electric charges repel; unlike charges attract.

TERM 3 · ENERGY & CHANGE

8. Electric circuits, resistance & investigations

Cell, battery & meters

A cell is one electrochemical source. A battery is two or more cells connected together.

An ammeter is connected in series. A voltmeter is connected in parallel across the component being measured.

In a simple series circuit, current is the same at all points. Across parallel branches, the potential difference is the same.

Resistance

Four important factors affecting a conductor's resistance:

materiallengththickness / cross-sectional areatemperature

Longer wire generally means more resistance. A thicker wire generally means less resistance.

Investigation: number of cells vs current

Independent variable: number of cells connected in series.

Dependent variable: current measured by the ammeter.

Hypothesis: if the number of cells in series increases, current will increase, provided the rest of the circuit is kept constant.

TERM 3 · ENERGY & CHANGE

9. Electrical safety, generation, national grid & cost

Safety at home

Protection includes fuses, circuit breakers and earth-leakage protection.

The earth wire gives fault current a low-resistance path to earth if a metal casing becomes live. This helps the protective device disconnect the supply and reduces electric-shock risk.

Coal-fired generation & grid

Coal provides heat→Water becomes steam→Steam turns turbine→Generator produces electricity

Electricity is transmitted at high voltage so the current can be lower for the same power transfer, reducing heating losses in transmission lines.

Cost calculation

Energy (kWh) = Power (kW) × time (h)
2 kW × 3 h = 6 kWh
Cost = 6 kWh × R3.50/kWh = R21.00
