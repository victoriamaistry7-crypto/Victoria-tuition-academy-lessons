EXPANSION • FOUNDATION

What does “expand” actually mean?

To expand, remove the brackets by multiplying the term outside by every term inside.

Example

4(x + 3)
4 × x = 4x→4 × 3 = 12→4x + 12
Most common mistake: writing 4x + 3. The 4 must multiply the 3 as well.
EXPANSION • DISTRIBUTIVE LAW

Use arrows so you never miss a term

1
Multiply the first term: 5 × 2x = 10x
2
Multiply the second term: 5 × (−3) = −15
3
Write the result: 10x − 15

Negative × positive

−3(2x) = −6x

Negative × negative

EXPAND → THEN SIMPLIFY

Do not stop too early

Example

3(2x + 5) + 4x
1
Expand: 6x + 15 + 4x
2
Combine like terms: 6x + 4x = 10x
3
Answer: 10x + 15
Exam habit: after expanding, scan the answer for like terms before you box it.

EXPANSION • TWO BRACKETS

Every term meets every term

When two brackets multiply, each term in the first bracket must multiply each term in the second.

(x + 2)(x + 5)
x·x = x²x·5 = 5x2·x = 2x2·5 = 10
x² + 5x + 2x + 10 = x² + 7x + 10
VISUAL METHOD

See the four products

The box is not another rule. It simply helps you make sure all four products appear.
TWO BRACKETS • SIGNS

Keep the signs attached to the terms

Worked example

(x − 4)(x + 2)
x² + 2x − 4x − 8
= x² − 2x − 8
Important: the term is −4, not just 4. Carry its sign through the multiplication.
SPECIAL PRODUCT

A bracket squared is not “square each term”

CORRECT
(x + 3)²

= (x + 3)(x + 3)

= x² + 6x + 9

COMMON ERROR
x² + 9

This loses the middle term 6x.

FACTORISATION • FOUNDATION

Now reverse the process

Expansion

5(x + 2)
→ 5x + 10

Factorisation

5x + 10
→ 5(x + 2)
Best check: expand your factorised answer. If you return to the original expression, your factorisation is correct.
FACTORISATION • COMMON FACTOR

Find what every term shares

12x + 18
1
The highest number dividing both 12 and 18 is 6.
2
12x ÷ 6 = 2x
3
18 ÷ 6 = 3
12x + 18 = 6(2x + 3)
COMMON FACTOR • VARIABLES

Numbers and letters can both be common

8x² + 12x

Number HCF: 4

Variable common to both terms: x

= 4x(2x + 3)
Why not just 4? Because if x is also common to every term, leaving it inside means the expression is not fully factorised.
FACTORISATION • NEGATIVES

Sometimes taking out a negative makes the bracket cleaner

−6x + 18

Take out −6:

−6(x − 3)

Check: −6·x = −6x and −6·(−3) = +18.

FACTORISATION • SPECIAL PATTERN

Difference of two squares

a² − b² = (a − b)(a + b)

It works

x² − 25

= x² − 5²

= (x − 5)(x + 5)

It does not fit

x² + 25

There is a plus sign, so it is not a difference of squares.

FACTOR FULLY

Sometimes one method is not enough

Example

18x² − 50
1
Take out HCF 2 → 2(9x² − 25)
2
Recognise squares: 9x² = (3x)² and 25 = 5²
3
2(3x − 5)(3x + 5)
Priority rule: always check for a common factor before looking for a special pattern.
FACTORISATION • TRINOMIALS

Find the pair

For a simple trinomial x² + bx + c, find two numbers that multiply to c and add to b.

x² + 7x + 12
Multiply to 123 × 4
Add to 73 + 4
= (x + 3)(x + 4)
TRINOMIALS • SIGNS

Use the last sign to guide you

Last term +
Middle +

Both signs are +.

x² + 7x + 12

Last term +
Middle −

Both signs are −.

x² − 7x + 12

Last term −

Signs are different.

x² + x − 12
METHOD SELECTION

What method should I use?

1. Is there a common factor?
Yes → take it out first.
2. Are there exactly two squared terms with subtraction?
Yes → difference of squares.
3. Are there three terms starting with x²?
Yes → try the trinomial pair method.
4. After factorising, can anything factor further?
Always check.
