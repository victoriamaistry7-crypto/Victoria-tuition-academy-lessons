What an electric cell does

The cell is an energy source, not a container of current.

An electric cell contains chemicals. Chemical reactions separate charge and create a potential difference between the terminals. When connected in a complete external circuit, charge can move and current can flow.

Key idea: a cell supplies energy that drives charge around a complete circuit.

Cell vs battery

These words are related but not identical.

Cell

One electrochemical energy source with a positive and a negative terminal.

Battery

Two or more cells connected together so they act as one source.

Inside a simple cell

Electrodes and electrolyte.

Electrode: conducting material through which charge enters or leaves the chemical system.

Electrolyte: substance containing mobile ions that allows charge transfer inside the chemical system.

Terminal: external positive or negative connection point.

Name the two electrodes shown.2 marks

Potential difference

The bridge into circuit analysis.

Potential difference tells us about the energy transferred per unit charge between two points. At this level, think of it as the electrical energy “push” supplied by a cell or battery.

Unit: volt, symbol V. A voltmeter measures potential difference and is connected across a component or source.
A cell is labelled 1.5 V. What does V mean, and what quantity is being stated?2 marks
